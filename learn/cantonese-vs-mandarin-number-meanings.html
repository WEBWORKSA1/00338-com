Cantonese vs Mandarin: Why the Same Number Can Be Lucky or Unlucky

By the 00338 Number Lab editorial team · Updated 2026-09-28 · 7 min read
Quick answer: Chinese number luck comes from sound-alike words, so it depends on dialect. Cantonese makes 2 "easy" (易) and 3 "life" (生), turning 28 and 38-free 3s very lucky; Mandarin can read 3 as "scatter" (散). Cantonese 5 (ng5) sounds like "not" (唔), so 58 reads "no prosperity".

Mandarin (Putonghua) is China's national language; Cantonese dominates Hong Kong, Macau, Guangdong and many overseas Chinese communities. Their pronunciations differ enough that the same number can send opposite messages.

Frequently asked questions

Why is 28 lucky in Hong Kong?

Cantonese 二八 (ji6 baat3) sounds like 易发 "easy prosperity".

Why is 58 unlucky in Cantonese?

五八 (ng5 baat3) sounds like 唔发 "not prosper".

Is 4 unlucky in both?

Yes. 四 sounds like 死 "death" in Mandarin (sǐ) and Cantonese (sei2).
