Why 4 Is Unlucky in Chinese Culture (and Where You'll Notice It)

By the 00338 Number Lab editorial team · Updated 2026-09-28 · 5 min read
Quick answer: 四 (sì) sounds almost the same as 死 (sǐ), "death", in Mandarin and Cantonese. That is why many buildings in Greater China skip the 4th, 14th and 24th floors, hospitals avoid room 4, and numbers containing 4 sell for less.

Fear of the number four — tetraphobia — is one of the most visible number beliefs in East Asia.

Where you'll see it

  • Buildings: many residential towers skip floors 4, 14, 24 and sometimes 40–49. One Hong Kong tower is famous for skipping over 40 floor numbers.
  • Hospitals & hotels: rooms ending in 4 are often skipped.
  • Prices: retailers avoid prices like 44 or 144.
  • Phone numbers & plates: sellers discount numbers with 4.
  • Gifts: never give four of anything — especially clocks, knives or pears.

Frequently asked questions

Do all Chinese people avoid 4?

Not all, but avoidance is widespread in property, phone numbers, plates, prices and gifts.

Is 4 ever lucky?

Occasionally, in phrases where the sound changes meaning — e.g. 3344 生生世世 "life after life" — and some almanacs list 4 as personally lucky for certain zodiac signs.

What about 14 and 24?

14 reads "will die" and 24 in Cantonese reads "easy death" — both avoided.
