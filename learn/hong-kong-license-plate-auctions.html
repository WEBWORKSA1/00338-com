Hong Kong Licence Plate Auctions: Record Prices & How Lucky Numbers Are Valued

By the 00338 Number Lab editorial team · Updated 2026-09-28 · 8 min read
Quick answer: Hong Kong's record plate is "W" at HK$26 million (2021). Numeric records include "28" (HK$18.1M, 2016), "18" (HK$16.5M, 2008) and "88" (HK$11.4M, 2025). Plates are sold by the Transport Department at public paddle auctions, with Lunar New Year sessions drawing the biggest bids.

How the auctions work

  • The Transport Department runs paddle-bid public auctions for traditional marks (e.g. "AB 1234") and personalised marks (up to 8 characters).
  • A typical session offers around 100 personalised and 220 traditional marks; personalised marks carry a HK$5,000 reserve price.
  • Winners must assign the mark to a vehicle within 12 months.
  • Special Lunar New Year auctions feature the most auspicious marks.

What makes a plate valuable?

  1. Brevity: single characters (W, H, S) and one-to-two digit numbers are the rarest.
  2. 8s and pairs: 8, 88, 18, 28, 168, 2288.
  3. No 4s: a 4 can cut value dramatically.
  4. Cantonese reading: because Hong Kong is Cantonese-speaking, 2 and 3 are strong positives.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the most expensive licence plate in Hong Kong?

"W", sold for HK$26 million in 2021, according to published auction records.

How do Hong Kong plate auctions work?

The Transport Department holds paddle-bid auctions for traditional and personalised marks; results are published after each session and plates must be assigned to a vehicle within 12 months.

Why are 18 and 28 so valuable?

In Cantonese, 18 reads 实发 "sure prosperity" and 28 reads 易发 "easy prosperity".
